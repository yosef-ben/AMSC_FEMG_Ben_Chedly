# Sequential ordering study

Which elimination ordering should the per-step factorization of the
Fisher-Kolmogorov solver use? The production class factorizes the
semi-implicit time-step matrix

```text
K = M + (dt/2) H - (dt/2) W(c_extrapolated)
```

once per time step with `Eigen::SparseLU`, whose default fill-reducing
ordering is COLAMD. K is symmetric, and the DoF numbering of the library
(interior chain nodes edge by edge, original vertices last) makes it a
bordered arrow matrix: one tridiagonal block per edge plus the vertex block
with the adjacency sparsity. This record measures what that structure is
worth against the generic heuristics, on the deterministic Corti-83
reference problem of the sequential-performance record (consistent mass,
semi-implicit scheme, dt = 0.2 over 20 years, D_e = w_e/max w, regional
rates of benchmark 21, entorhinal seed 0.10 over the 0.01 background).

## Setup

`test_ordering_study` (test_problem/performance) replays the production
time loop through the public accessors of the class, without touching the
library, and times every phase of all 100 steps for one solver variant at a
time: matrix rebuild (reaction weight assembly and matrix combinations),
factorization as the production code performs it (`compute()`, symbolic
analysis included, because the loop rebuilds the matrix at every step),
numeric-only refactorization (the pattern is fixed, so a solver that keeps
its symbolic analysis pays only this), and the triangular solves. Variants:

```text
lu_colamd     Eigen::SparseLU + COLAMD          (the production configuration)
lu_natural    Eigen::SparseLU + natural order   (interiors first, vertices last)
lu_amd        Eigen::SparseLU + AMD
ldlt_amd      Eigen::SimplicialLDLT + AMD       (K is symmetric)
ldlt_natural  Eigen::SimplicialLDLT + natural
lu_rcm        reverse Cuthill-McKee permutation, then SparseLU + natural
```

Meshes: the fornari83 graph at 1, 2, 4, 8, 16, 32 and 64 cells per edge (83
to 71273 unknowns; the three refined meshes are new outputs of the same
prepare script, the stored ones are regenerated byte-identical). Threads
pinned to one. `results/validation.csv`: the replayed lu_colamd loop
reproduces the `solve()` of the library class with difference exactly zero
at 1, 2, 4 and 8 cells per edge, so every measurement refers to the
verified sequential solver, which is not modified.

## What the study shows

1. **The production default is the anomaly.** COLAMD's fill is
   non-monotonic in the problem size: 182208 nonzeros at 1213 unknowns
   against 77828 at 3473. This reproduces, inside one process, the
   two-element anomaly of the sequential-performance record (benchmark 22):
   the heuristic misjudges the hub-dominated vertex block at that size, not
   the problem.
2. **The natural ordering is the structure.** Interiors-first is implicit
   static condensation: eliminating the chains produces no fill outside the
   vertex block, so `lu_natural` beats COLAMD at every refined size and
   `ldlt_natural` (same ordering, symmetry exploited) is the best variant
   everywhere: 44x the production per-step time at 2 cells, 11x at 8, 6.7x
   at 64.
3. **Bandwidth is the wrong objective here.** RCM reduces the bandwidth
   from 71269 to 937 and still produces 250x the fill of the natural
   ordering, because a banded envelope is dense inside while the arrow
   matrix is not. AMD essentially rediscovers the natural elimination (its
   LDLT fill matches to 502 nonzeros) but pays its ordering computation at
   every step under the production rebuild-and-compute pattern.
4. **Two further margins for the next stages.** The numeric-only
   refactorization column shows what reusing the symbolic analysis buys
   (6.1 to 3.7 ms at 64 cells for the winner); and once the factorization
   costs 6 ms, the matrix rebuild (14 ms at 64 cells) is the new bottleneck,
   which is where the assembly parallelization and the explicit per-edge
   condensation of the following stages take over.

Every variant reproduces the production trajectory within 3.2e-14 in the
maximum norm over all 100 steps (`max_diff_vs_colamd`).

## Reference choice for the next stage

`ldlt_natural`: the symmetric factorization on the library's own
interiors-first ordering. It is the fastest at every size, its ordering is
the one the explicit condensation of the next stage makes exact, and it
keeps the sequential baseline honest: parallel speedups will be measured
against it, not against the COLAMD configuration it replaces.
